# Three preliminary findings

These are leads from the IRaMuTeQ class profiles, not validated frame counts. Percentages below are shares of classified text segments within each separate analysis; classes and shares should not be compared as if they were identical categories across corpora.

1. **Ecological and infrastructure pressures appear across the cases.** Energy/resource use is a prominent French class both with and without Les Échos (14.1% and 14.3%). In Ireland, data centres, electricity and the grid form the largest class (17.4%); a further class links fossil fuels and climate (8.1%). This makes ecology and energy a cross-national strand, not an obvious Ireland-only concern.

2. **French sovereignty may surface indirectly through regulation and geopolitics.** The French profiles separate EU law/regulation (6.7% with Les Échos) and China–Europe geopolitics (10.9%); without Les Échos, digital governance and EU regulation form a class (8%). Ireland also has an EU regulation class (7.7%). These are plausible sovereignty-related contexts, but IRaMuTeQ does not show that these passages make a sovereignty claim; segment-level reading and coding are needed.

3. **Investment, markets and AI infrastructure cut across the proposed contrast.** The French profiles include investment/finance and semiconductor/market classes in both runs; without Les Échos, AI models/cloud/computing are especially visible (16.1%). Ireland also surfaces investment, chips and technology-company earnings. Les Échos changes the French class structure, especially its distinct finance and company-results classes, so newspaper composition should be treated as part of the comparison.

**Next step for the RQ:** compare sampled segments in the energy/resource and regulation/geopolitics classes, coding whether they connect data centres to sustainability, digital sovereignty, both, or neither. The Irish solution classified 81.5% of segments (versus 96–97% in France), so its class pattern needs extra caution. The French no-Les-Échos run also contains a likely function-word/noise class (18.9%), excluded from the substantive reading above.
